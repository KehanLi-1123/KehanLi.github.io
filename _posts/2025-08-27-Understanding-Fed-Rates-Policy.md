## What Does It Mean When Powell Says the Fed Will Cut Rates by 25 bps?

When Jay Powell announces that the Fed is “cutting rates by 25 basis points,” he is referring to the **federal funds target range**. This is the range of interest rates at which commercial banks lend reserves to each other overnight. For example, the Fed might lower the target range from **5.25–5.50%** to **5.00–5.25%**.

### Which rate is being cut?
- The **Federal Funds Rate (FFR)** target range is the official policy tool.  
- Markets often quote the **midpoint** of this range (e.g. 5.375% → 5.125%).  
- This rate is the anchor for short-term funding markets and heavily influences Treasury yields, mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs, and global financial conditions.

### How does the Fed actually "cut" the rate?
The Fed does not directly command banks to lend at a certain rate. Instead, it uses tools to steer the market rate into the new target range:

1. **Interest on Reserve Balances (IORB):**  
   The Fed pays interest on the reserves that commercial banks hold at the Fed. By adjusting this rate downward, it lowers the floor for what banks are willing to accept in the market.

2. **Overnight Reverse Repo Facility (ON RRP):**  
   The Fed offers reverse repos to money market funds and others. Adjusting this rate ensures non-banks also face the new lower floor for short-term lending.

3. **Open Market Operations (historically):**  
   Before 2008, the Fed actively added or drained reserves in the banking system to push the fed funds rate toward the target. Today, because reserves are abundant, the Fed relies mainly on IORB and ON RRP.

Through these mechanisms, the **effective federal funds rate** (EFFR), which is the actual market rate, is guided into the new lower target range.

### Why does this matter?
- Lowering the federal funds target range by 25 bps reduces short-term borrowing costs.  
- It transmits through the yield curve, lowering Treasury yields, mortgage rates, and loan rates for businesses and consumers.  
- This in turn supports growth and employment, though with a lag, while also influencing inflation expectations.

---

**In short:**  
When Powell says the Fed is cutting rates by 25 bps, it means the **overnight federal funds target range** is reduced by a quarter of a percentage point. The Fed enforces this by lowering the interest it pays on reserves and adjusting its money market facilities, thereby guiding the effective fed funds rate down into the new range.
