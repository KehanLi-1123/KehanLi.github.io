## Accumulating (Acc) ETFs as Total-Return Instruments

### 1. Definition and Core Property

An **Accumulating (Acc) ETF** reinvests all cash income generated by its underlying assets (e.g. bond coupons or equity dividends) back into the fund, rather than distributing this income to investors.

As a result, the investor’s realised return is **entirely reflected in the Net Asset Value (NAV)** evolution.

Formally, for an Acc ETF held from time $0$ to $T$:
$$
\text{Total Return}_{0,T} \;=\; \frac{NAV_T - NAV_0}{NAV_0}
$$

There is **no separate cash-flow leg** from the investor’s perspective.

---

### 2. Comparison with Distributing Instruments

For a distributing bond or a distributing ETF, total return decomposes into price return and income return:
$$
\text{Total Return} 
\;=\; \frac{P_T - P_0}{P_0} 
\;+\; \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N C_i}{P_0}
$$
where $C_i$ are coupon or dividend payments.

For an **Acc ETF**, income is reinvested immediately, so:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^N C_i \;\;\text{is embedded in}\;\; NAV_T
$$

Hence:
$$
\text{Total Return}_{\text{Acc ETF}} \;=\; \frac{NAV_T - NAV_0}{NAV_0}
$$

This behaviour is mathematically equivalent to:
- A **total-return index**
- A **zero-coupon bond**
- A **total-return swap** on the underlying basket

---

### 3. Comparison with Holding a Single Coupon Bond

| Instrument | Coupon Paid | Price Drop on Coupon Date | Reinvestment Needed | Return Type |
|-----------|------------|----------------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Coupon bond | Yes | Yes | Yes | Split (income + price) |
| Distributing ETF | Yes | Yes | Yes | Split |
| **Accumulating ETF (Acc)** | No (reinvested internally) | No | No | **Total return** |
| Zero-coupon bond | No | No | No | Total return |

Key interpretation:

- Coupon bonds and distributing ETFs require **external reinvestment** to achieve total return.
- Acc ETFs internalise reinvestment, so the **entire return is capitalised into NAV**.
- Zero-coupon bonds and Acc ETFs therefore share the same **economic return structure**, despite different instruments.

---

### 4. NAV Dynamics and Compounding

Let:
- $r_t$ be the instantaneous total return of the underlying portfolio
- $NAV_t$ be the ETF NAV

Then:
$$
\frac{dNAV_t}{NAV_t} \;=\; r_t \, dt
$$

Carry, income, and roll-down effects are **continuously compounded**, producing smooth NAV evolution without ex-dividend price drops.

---

### 5. DV01 and Duration Interpretation

From a rates-risk perspective, an Acc bond ETF behaves as a **self-reinvesting bond portfolio**.

Let:
- $V$ be the ETF NAV
- $y$ be the relevant yield level

Then:
$$
DV01 \;\approx\; - \frac{\partial V}{\partial y} \times 10^{-4}
$$

Implications:
- Coupon reinvestment preserves duration exposure
- DV01 evolves smoothly through time
- No mechanical DV01 loss due to coupon payments
- Duration decay occurs only via:
  - Passage of time
  - Index rebalancing rules

---

### 6. Carry and Roll-Down Embedded in NAV

Expected short-horizon return decomposes as:
$$
\mathbb{E}[r] 
\;\approx\; 
\text{Carry} 
\;+\; 
\text{Roll-Down} 
\;+\; 
\text{Yield Change Effect}
$$

Because income is reinvested:
- Carry directly increases NAV
- Roll-down is fully reflected in price appreciation
- No cash drag or reinvestment timing mismatch

This provides **clean exposure to curve level and shape**.

---

### 7. Summary

> **An Acc ETF behaves like a total-return instrument because all income is reinvested internally, causing carry and roll-down to be capitalised into NAV rather than paid out as cash.**

From a rates perspective, this results in:
- Smooth NAV evolution  
- Stable DV01 exposure  
- Clean duration, carry, and roll-down attribution
