## 1. Introduction

Accumulating (Acc) ETFs are often described as **behaving like total-return instruments**.  
This note explains what that means from a **cash-flow**, **valuation**, and **rates-risk** perspective.

Particular emphasis is placed on:
- Net Asset Value (NAV) dynamics  
- Carry and roll-down  
- Duration and DV01 intuition  
- A **numerical 20-year US Treasury example**

---

## 2. Definition and Core Property

An **Accumulating (Acc) ETF** reinvests all cash income generated by its underlying assets back into the fund, rather than distributing this income to investors.

As a result, the investor’s realised return is **fully reflected in the Net Asset Value (NAV)**.

For an Acc ETF held from time zero to time T, the total return is:

$$
\text{Total Return}_{0,T}
=
\frac{NAV_T - NAV_0}{NAV_0}
$$

There is **no separate cash-flow leg** from the investor’s perspective.

## 3. Comparison with Distributing Instruments

For a distributing bond or ETF, total return decomposes into price return and income return:

$$
\text{Total Return}
=
\frac{P_T - P_0}{P_0}
+
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i}{P_0}
$$

The cash flows received over the holding period are:

$$
C_1, C_2, \dots, C_N
$$

For an **Acc ETF**, all income is reinvested internally, so cumulative income is embedded in the terminal NAV:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i
\;\;\text{is embedded in}\;\;
NAV_T
$$

Hence:

$$
\text{Total Return}_{\text{Acc ETF}}
=
\frac{NAV_T - NAV_0}{NAV_0}
$$

This behaviour is economically equivalent to:
- A total-return index  
- A zero-coupon bond  
- A total-return swap on the underlying basket  

---

## 4. Comparison with Holding a Single Coupon Bond

| Instrument | Coupon Paid | Price Drop on Coupon Date | Reinvestment Needed | Return Type |
|-----------|------------|----------------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Coupon bond | Yes | Yes | Yes | Split (income + price) |
| Distributing ETF | Yes | Yes | Yes | Split |
| **Accumulating ETF (Acc)** | No (reinvested internally) | No | No | **Total return** |
| Zero-coupon bond | No | No | No | Total return |

## 5. NAV Dynamics and Compounding

Let the instantaneous total return of the underlying portfolio be:

$$
r_t
$$

and the ETF Net Asset Value be:

$$
NAV_t
$$

Then the NAV evolves as:

$$
\frac{dNAV_t}{NAV_t}
=
r_t \, dt
$$

Carry, income, and roll-down are **continuously capitalised**, producing smooth NAV growth without ex-dividend effects.

---

## 6. DV01 and Duration Interpretation

Let the ETF value be:

$$
V
$$

and the relevant yield level be:

$$
y
$$

The DV01 is approximated by:

$$
DV01
\approx
- \frac{\partial V}{\partial y}
\times 10^{-4}
$$

Key implications:
- No mechanical DV01 loss from coupon payments
- Duration decays only via time and index rebalancing
- Risk behaves like a rolling bond ladder

## 7. Carry and Roll-Down Embedded in NAV

### 7.1 Carry

Let the continuously compounded yield of the bond portfolio be:

$$
y
$$

Over a short horizon:

$$
\Delta t
$$

with yields unchanged, carry is approximately:

$$
\text{Carry}
\approx
y \cdot \Delta t
$$

For portfolio value:

$$
P_0
$$

the price impact from carry is:

$$
\Delta P_{\text{carry}}
\approx
P_0 \cdot y \cdot \Delta t
$$

Carry represents **income earned simply by holding the bond**, assuming no market movement.

---

### 7.2 Roll-Down

Let the zero-coupon yield curve be:

$$
y(T)
$$

A bond with maturity:

$$
T
$$

after time:

$$
\Delta t
$$

moves to maturity:

$$
T - \Delta t
$$

If the curve is upward sloping:

$$
y(T - \Delta t) < y(T)
$$

The roll-down price effect is approximated by:

$$
\Delta P_{\text{roll}}
\approx
- \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
\cdot
\left[ y(T - \Delta t) - y(T) \right]
$$

Roll-down represents **capital gains from ageing along the yield curve**, even if the curve itself does not move.

---

### 7.3 Combined Return Decomposition

The expected short-horizon return satisfies:

$$
\mathbb{E}[r]
\approx
\text{Carry}
+
\text{Roll-Down}
+
\text{Yield Change Effect}
$$

In Acc ETFs, carry and roll-down are **fully embedded in NAV**.

## 8. Numerical Example: 20-Year US Treasury

Assumptions:
- 20-year Treasury yield: 4.00 percent  
- Holding horizon: one year  
- Yield curve roll-down from 20Y to 19Y: 8 basis points  
- Modified duration: 16  
- No yield level change  
- Convexity ignored  

---

### Numerical Decomposition

| Component | Assumption | Calculation Logic | Contribution |
|---------|-----------|------------------|--------------|
| Carry | Yield = 4.00% | Earned by holding for one year | 4.00% |
| Roll-down | Curve slope = −8 bp | Duration × yield change | 1.28% |
| Yield change | No shift | Assumed zero | 0.00% |
| **Total expected return** | — | Carry + roll-down | **5.28%** |

---

### Mathematical Representation

Carry contribution:

$$
\text{Carry}
\approx
0.0400
$$

Roll-down yield change:

$$
\Delta y
=
-0.0008
$$

Roll-down price effect:

$$
\text{Roll-Down}
\approx
16
\times
0.0008
=
0.0128
$$

Total expected return:

$$
\mathbb{E}[r]
\approx
0.0400
+
0.0128
=
0.0528
$$

All of this return is realised through **NAV appreciation** in an Acc ETF.

---

## 9. Summary

> **Carry is income earned by holding a bond assuming yields are unchanged.  
> Roll-down is capital gain or loss from ageing along the yield curve.**

In an Acc ETF:
- Both effects are fully capitalised into NAV  
- Returns behave like a total-return instrument  
- DV01 and duration evolve smoothly  

This makes Acc ETFs particularly suitable for **medium-horizon rate and curve strategies**.
