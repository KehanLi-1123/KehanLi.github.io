## 🧭 Overview

**Asset–Liability Management (ALM)** is the discipline of managing a bank’s balance-sheet risks arising from mismatches between **assets** (loans, securities) and **liabilities** (deposits, borrowings).

Its core objectives are:

- **Economic Value of Equity (EVE) management** → focus on *value sensitivity* (via **Duration** or **DV01**)
- **Net Interest Income (NII) management** → focus on *earnings sensitivity* (via **NII01**)

---

## 🧩 Example Balance Sheet Mismatch

| Item | Type | Amount | Average Maturity | Rate Type | DV01 (per bp) |
|------|------|---------|------------------|------------|----------------|
| 3-year fixed-rate bond | Asset | £100 m | 3 years | Receives **fixed** | **+£28 k** |
| 6-month customer deposit | Liability | £100 m | 0.5 year | Pays **fixed** | **–£3 k** |

**Net DV01 = +25 k**

→ If market rates rise by 1 bp, the bank’s **EVE falls by £25 k**.  
The balance sheet is **long duration** — it loses value when rates rise.

---

## 🧾 Step 1 – DV01-Based ALM (EVE Focus)

To neutralize this exposure, ALM uses an **interest-rate swap (IRS)**.

| Parameter | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Notional | £90 m |
| Swap type | **Pay fixed, receive floating** |
| Term / Maturity | **3 years** (matches the bond) |
| Fixed leg | 4.00 % semi-annual payments (30/360) |
| Floating leg | 6M SONIA or SOFR (ACT/360) |
| Purpose | Converts part of fixed-rate assets into floating exposure |

---

## 🧮 DV01 Calculation

For a fixed-for-floating swap, the **DV01** (dollar value of one basis point) measures the PV change per 1 bp parallel shift in the curve:

$$
\text{DV01}_{\text{swap}} = N \times \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(t_i)\,\Delta_i \,/\, 10{,}000
$$

where:
- \( N \): notional (£90 m)  
- \( P(t_i) \): discount factor to the i-th payment date  
- \( \Delta_i \): accrual fraction (e.g., 0.5 for semi-annual)  
- \( n \): number of fixed coupons

---

### Example (Flat 4% curve)

| i | \(t_i\) (yrs) | \(P(t_i)\) | \(\Delta_i\) | \(P(t_i)\Delta_i\) |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 0.5 | 0.980 | 0.5 | 0.490 |
| 2 | 1.0 | 0.961 | 0.5 | 0.481 |
| 3 | 1.5 | 0.942 | 0.5 | 0.471 |
| 4 | 2.0 | 0.923 | 0.5 | 0.462 |
| 5 | 2.5 | 0.905 | 0.5 | 0.452 |
| 6 | 3.0 | 0.888 | 0.5 | 0.444 |
| **Sum** | | | | **2.80** |

$$
\text{DV01}_{\text{swap}} = 90{,}000{,}000 \times 2.80 / 10{,}000 = £25{,}200
$$

Because the bank **pays fixed**, the swap DV01 is **–£25 k** (it gains when rates rise).

---

### Net DV01 After Hedging

| Component | DV01 | Sign |
|------------|------|------|
| 3-year fixed bond | +28 k | Long rates |
| 6-month deposit | –3 k | Short rates |
| **Pay-fixed swap (3y)** | –25 k | Short rates |
| **Net** | ≈ 0 | Duration-neutral |

After hedging, the bank’s **EVE** is stable to small rate shifts — a textbook **duration / DV01 matching**.

---

## ⚙️ Deposit Rate Behavior

| Deposit type | Bank pays | Repricing | Duration | DV01 |
|---------------|------------|------------|-----------|------|
| 6-month term | Fixed | Every 6m | Short | Small negative |
| Overnight | Floating | Daily | ≈ 0 | ≈ 0 |

Thus:
- The **bond (3y fixed)** is long duration  
- The **deposit (6m fixed)** is short duration  
→ The bank is **long rates**, so ALM adds a **pay-fixed swap** to shorten duration.

---

## 🧮 Summary Formulas (EVE focus)

Duration / DV01 neutralization:

$$
\text{DV01(Assets)} \approx \text{DV01(Liabilities)} + \text{DV01(Hedges)}
$$

## 💰 Step 2 – NII-Based ALM (Earnings Focus)

Now, let’s switch focus from *value* to *earnings*.  
We look at **cashflows within a 12-month horizon**.

### Setup

| Item | Type | Maturity | Repricing | NII effect within 12m |
|------|------|-----------|------------|-----------------------|
| 3-year fixed bond | Asset | 3y | No | **0** |
| 6-month fixed deposit | Liability | 0.5y | Yes, at 6m | **Refixes once** |

---

### NII01 (per 1 bp rate move)

Let:
- \( N = 100{,}000{,}000 \)
- \( \Delta r = 0.0001 \)
- Only the second 6 months (after refix) are affected: \( 0.5 \) year

**Liability NII01** (bank pays interest):

$$
\text{NII01}_{\text{deposit}} = -\, N \times \Delta r \times 0.5
$$

$$
= -\,100{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0001 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{-£5{,}000}
$$

**Asset NII01 (3y fixed):**

$$
\text{NII01}_{\text{bond}} \approx 0
$$

**Total NII01 (unhedged):**

$$
\text{NII01}_{\text{total}} = -£5{,}000
$$

→ If rates rise 1 bp, **next-12-month NII decreases by £5k**.  
The deposit reprices sooner than the asset, increasing funding costs.

---

### NII Hedge: Forward-Starting Swap (6x12)

To offset that, the ALM can use a **6x12 pay-fixed / receive-floating IRS**.

| Parameter | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Start | in 6 months |
| End | in 12 months |
| Type | **Pay fixed, receive floating** |
| Notional | To offset £5k/bp |
| Purpose | Receive floating interest during months 7–12 to offset higher deposit cost |

**Hedge NII01:**

$$
\text{NII01}_{\text{hedge}} = +\, N_h \times \Delta r \times 0.5
$$

Setting:

$$
\text{NII01}_{\text{hedge}} = +£5{,}000 \quad \Rightarrow \quad
N_h = \frac{5{,}000}{0.0001 \times 0.5} = \mathbf{£100{,}000{,}000}
$$

So a **6x12 pay-fixed / receive-floating** swap on **£100m notional** fully offsets the **NII01** impact.

---

## ⚙️ Refinements for Realistic ALM

1. **Deposit beta (β)**  
   If only a fraction of deposit rates move with market rates:
   $$
   \text{NII01}_{\text{deposit}} = -\, \beta \, N \times \Delta r \times 0.5
   $$
   Then hedge notional becomes \( N_h = \beta \times £100m \).

2. **Deposit floors / asymmetry**  
   If deposit rates have a 0% floor, downward NII sensitivity is muted.

3. **Portfolio aggregation**  
   Real ALM aggregates all items repricing within 12m:
   $$
   \text{NII01} = \sum_{j} s_j \, \beta_j \, N_j \times \frac{\text{days repricing within horizon}}{360} \times 10^{-4}
   $$
   where \( s_j = +1 \) for assets (receive), \( s_j = -1 \) for liabilities (pay).

4. **Dual metrics**  
   Banks often manage both:
   - **EVE sensitivity** (via long swaps → duration hedge)
   - **NII sensitivity** (via short, forward-starting swaps → earnings hedge)

## 🧠 Conceptual Comparison

| Aspect | EVE / DV01-based ALM | NII-based ALM |
|--------|----------------------|----------------|
| Horizon | Long-term (economic value) | Short-term (12m earnings) |
| Metric | Duration / DV01 | NII01 |
| Objective | Stable **economic value** | Stable **net interest margin** |
| Typical hedge | 3y–10y swaps | FRAs / 3–12m forward swaps |
| Impact | Balance sheet value | Annual income |
| Repricing considered | Entire term structure | Only within next 12 months |

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- **DV01-based ALM**: stabilizes *EVE* by aligning the durations of assets and liabilities.  
  $$
  \text{DV01(Assets)} \approx \text{DV01(Liabilities)} + \text{DV01(Hedges)}
  $$

- **NII-based ALM**: stabilizes *earnings* by matching refixing schedules within a horizon.  
  $$
  \text{NII01(Assets)} \approx \text{NII01(Liabilities)}
  $$

- **3y fixed asset + 6m deposit** → long duration, short NII.  
  - Hedge DV01: **3y pay-fixed swap**  
  - Hedge NII: **6x12 pay-fixed swap**  

- Real ALM integrates both perspectives under **IRRBB (Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book)**.

## 💡 Final Intuition

- ALM ≠ trading — it’s **internal risk stabilization**.  
- **EVE focus** = market value neutrality.  
- **NII focus** = earnings neutrality.  
- Both use swaps, FRAs, and term funding to ensure the bank remains resilient across interest-rate cycles.
