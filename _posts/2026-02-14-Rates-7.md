We first analyze a standard EUR/USD **Mark-to-Market (MtM) Cross-Currency Basis Swap** with the following characteristics:
* **Pay Leg:** USD Principal + USD SOFR
* **Receive Leg:** EUR Principal + (EUR €STR + Spread)
* **Collateral (CSA):** USD (Discounting based on USD SOFR)

---

## 1. The Sign of the Spread (The Basis)

In the standard interbank market for EUR/USD, the spread (denoted as $$\gamma$$) is almost always **negative**.

### The Economic Reason: "Dollar Shortage"
Since 2008, there has been a persistent global demand for USD. Because USD is the "hub" currency for global trade, market participants are willing to pay a premium to borrow USD. 

In a swap where you **receive** the foreign currency (EUR) and **pay** the base currency (USD), you are essentially a "provider" of USD to the market. To compensate for the high demand for USD, the counterparty pays you a **lower** interest rate on the EUR you receive.

$$\text{Rate Received} = \text{€STR} + \gamma \quad (\text{where } \gamma < 0)$$

---

## 2. Pricing and Valuation Formula

The Net Present Value ($$NPV$$) of the swap in USD terms is the difference between the present value of the two legs, converted at the Spot FX rate ($$S_0$$).

### The USD Leg (Pay Leg)
Its value is discounted using the USD SOFR curve:
$$PV_{USD} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{CF_{USD, i}}{(1 + r_{SOFR, i})^{t_i}} + \frac{Principal_{USD}}{(1 + r_{SOFR, n})^{t_n}}$$

### The EUR Leg (Receive Leg)
This leg is discounted using the **Basis-Adjusted CSA Curve**. Note that the spread $$\gamma$$ is included in the cash flow projection:
$$PV_{EUR} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{Principal_{EUR} \times (\text{€STR}_i + \gamma) \times \Delta t_i}{(1 + r_{Basis\_Adj, i})^{t_i}} + \frac{Principal_{EUR}}{(1 + r_{Basis\_Adj, n})^{t_n}}$$

### Total NPV
$$NPV_{Total} = (PV_{EUR} \times S_0) - PV_{USD}$$

---

## 3. Impact of Basis Volatility

Because you are **receiving** the basis ($$€STR + \gamma$$), your position has specific sensitivities (Greeks):

| Market Move | Direction | Impact on your MtM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basis becomes more negative** | $$-25 \text{bps} \to -50 \text{bps}$$ | **Loss** (You receive a lower rate) |
| **Basis becomes less negative** | $$-25 \text{bps} \to -10 \text{bps}$$ | **Gain** (You receive a higher rate) |

---

## 4. The Mathematical "Holy Trinity" of Calibration

When the CSA specifies USD SOFR as collateral, we must calibrate a specific EUR CSA curve. The core of this process is defined by these three mathematical pillars:

### I. The Construction Equation (Definition)
The EUR CSA curve is defined in discount factor space as a spread over the native EUR curve:
$$DF_{EUR}^{CSA}(T) = DF_{EUR}^{€STR}(T) \times \exp\left(-\int_0^T \gamma(t) dt\right)$$
This ensures that if the basis spread $$\gamma$$ were zero, we would revert to the native €STR discounting.

### II. The Equilibrium Condition (No-Arbitrage)
At inception, a market basis swap is a par instrument, meaning its value in the base currency (USD) is zero:
$$0 = \text{NPV}_{USD\_Leg} - S_0 \times \text{NPV}_{EUR\_Leg}$$

### III. The Calibration Equation (The Solver)
This is the operational formula used in the bootstrapper to solve for unknown discount factors pillar-by-pillar:
$$0 = \sum_{k=1}^m \text{SOFR}_k \cdot DF_{USD}^{SOFR}(T_k) - S_0 \left[ \sum_{k=1}^m (\text{€STR}_k + \bar{\gamma}_j) \cdot DF_{EUR}^{CSA}(T_k) \right]$$



---

## 5. Summary of the Calibration Flow

1. **Fix the Anchor:** Calibrate the USD SOFR curve and EUR €STR curve independently from their respective OIS markets.
2. **Input Market Basis:** Obtain the quoted market basis $$\bar{\gamma}_j$$for a specific maturity$$T_j$$.
3. **Bootstrap:** Use the **Calibration Equation** to solve for the EUR CSA discount factor ($$DF_{EUR}^{CSA}$$) at that maturity.
4. **Interpolate:** Use the **Construction Equation** to back out the instantaneous basis function $$\gamma(t)$$, typically assuming a piecewise constant structure.
