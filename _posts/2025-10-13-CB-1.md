## 📘 Overview

**NII01** — *Net Interest Income 01* — measures the change in a bank’s **projected net interest income** (NII) for a **1 basis point (0.01%) parallel shift** in interest rates.

It’s the **accrual-based counterpart** of DV01 and EVE01, focusing on *earnings* rather than *valuation*.  

$$
\text{NII01} = \frac{\partial (\text{Net Interest Income})}{\partial r} \times 10^{-4}
$$

---

## 🧩 Concept and Definition

**Net Interest Income (NII)** is the difference between **interest income** (from loans, securities) and **interest expense** (on deposits, funding), typically over a **12-month horizon**.

**NII01** therefore measures how much that future income changes if market rates rise or fall by 1 bp.

Example intuition:
> If rates rise by 1 bp and your balance sheet earns slightly more on assets than it pays on liabilities,  
> your NII01 will be positive — indicating higher income.

---

## 💡 Why “Only Consider Principal”

When calculating NII01, documents often specify:  
> *“Only consider the principal.”*

This reflects the economic mechanism:

$$
\text{Interest Income} = \text{Principal} \times \text{Rate} \times \text{Time}
$$

A small rate shift changes **only the interest accrued on the principal** — not the mark-to-market value of the loan.  
Hence, NII01 captures the **incremental change in cash interest**, not revaluation gains or losses.

What’s excluded:
- Fair-value (PV) changes → counted in **EVE01**, not NII01  
- Non-interest income and fees  
- Accrued interest already realized  

---

## 🕒 Time Horizon Intuition

Banks generally measure NII01 over **12 months**, matching the **accounting and budgeting cycle**.  
However, each product contributes only for the period it remains **outstanding or reprices**.

For a 3-month loan:

$$
\text{NII01} = \text{Principal} \times 0.0001 \times 0.25
$$

Example:  
- Principal = £100 m  
- Horizon = 3 months = 0.25 years  

$$
\text{NII01} = 100{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0001 \times 0.25 = £2{,}500
$$

Shorter tenor → smaller sensitivity.

---

## 🧮 Comparison of Risk Metrics

| Metric | Basis | Measures | Horizon | Typical Use |
|---------|--------|-----------|----------|--------------|
| **DV01** | PV | Change in instrument value per 1 bp | Lifetime | Trading book |
| **EVE01** | PV | Change in economic value of equity | Lifetime | Banking book (IRRBB) |
| **NII01** | Accrual | Change in projected net interest income | ~12 months | Banking book (P&L sensitivity) |

---

## ⚙️ Role of Derivatives

Derivatives such as swaps, FRAs, or options generally **don’t belong in NII01**, because:

1. They don’t accrue interest on a principal balance.  
2. Their exposure is mark-to-market, not accrual-based.  
3. Including them would double-count rate sensitivity already reflected in DV01 or EVE01.

The only exception is **banking-book cash-flow hedges**, where the swap’s floating leg directly offsets the variability of an asset or liability’s interest cash flows under IFRS 9 hedge accounting.

Even then, the swap’s effect is included **only for its cash-flow impact**, not its valuation change.

---

## 🧭 Economic Interpretation

| Book | Main Metric | Perspective | Intuition |
|------|--------------|--------------|------------|
| **Trading Book** | DV01 | Fair-value | “How much does PV change per 1 bp?” |
| **Banking Book (Value)** | EVE01 | Economic value | “How does the long-term equity value change per 1 bp?” |
| **Banking Book (Earnings)** | NII01 | Income accrual | “How much will next year’s interest earnings change per 1 bp?” |

In short:

> **NII01** = short-term *profit sensitivity*  
> **EVE01** = long-term *capital sensitivity*  
> **DV01** = mark-to-market *valuation sensitivity*

---

## ✅ Summary

- **NII01** quantifies how a 1 bp rate move affects *future interest earnings*, not portfolio value.  
- It depends only on **principal × rate × time** — hence “principal only”.  
- The horizon is typically **12 months**, but shorter-term loans contribute proportionally.  
- Derivatives are excluded unless treated as **cash-flow hedges**.  
- For mark-to-market exposures, **EVE01 or DV01** are the correct measures.
