## Overview

The **traffic light model** is a regulatory framework proposed by the Basel Committee to backtest **Value-at-Risk (VaR)** models.  
It classifies a model’s performance into **green, yellow, or red zones** depending on the number of *exceptions* observed in backtesting.

- An **exception** occurs when the realized loss on a day exceeds the predicted 1-day 99% VaR.  
- The framework is typically applied over a **250-trading-day window** (≈ 1 year).

---

## 1. Statistical Setup

Suppose we have:

- Confidence level: 99%  
- Backtesting horizon: 250 trading days  
- Exceptions:  

\[
\text{Exception}_t = \mathbf{1}\{\text{Loss}_t > \text{VaR}_t\}, \quad
N = \sum_{t=1}^{250} \text{Exception}_t
\]

Under a correct VaR model, the number of exceptions follows a **binomial distribution**:

\[
N \sim \text{Binomial}(n=250, p=0.01)
\]

with expectation:

\[
\mathbb{E}[N] = 2.5
\]

---

## 2. Basel Traffic Light Zones

The Basel Committee defines thresholds for the number of exceptions:

- **Green zone:** \(0 \leq N \leq 4\)  
  → Model is consistent with 99% VaR.  
- **Yellow zone:** \(5 \leq N \leq 9\)  
  → Model underestimates risk somewhat; subject to supervisory review.  
- **Red zone:** \(N \geq 10\)  
  → Model is unreliable; strong supervisory action.

---

## 3. Probabilities Under a Correct Model

Using the binomial distribution \( \text{Binomial}(250,0.01) \):

- **Green zone (0–4 exceptions):**  
  \(\mathbb{P}(N \leq 4) \approx 89.2\%\)

- **Yellow zone (5–9 exceptions):**  
  \(\mathbb{P}(5 \leq N \leq 9) \approx 10.8\%\)

- **Red zone (≥10 exceptions):**  
  \(\mathbb{P}(N \geq 10) \approx 0.025\%\)  
  (about 1 in 4,000)

Thus, under a well-specified 99% VaR, we expect to fall in the **green zone most of the time**, occasionally in **yellow**, and *almost never* in **red**.

---

## 4. Capital Implications

Regulatory capital for market risk is scaled by a **multiplier**:

\[
k = 3 + \text{penalty}(N)
\]

- If \(N \leq 4\), then \(k = 3\).  
- For each step into the yellow/red zones, the penalty increases (up to a maximum of 1.0).  
- If \(N \geq 10\), \(k = 4\).

This penalizes models that produce too many exceptions by requiring banks to hold more capital.

---

## 5. Example

Suppose you backtest a 1-day 99% VaR model with 250 daily P&L observations:

- Observed exceptions: \(N = 7\)  
- Zone: **Yellow**  
- Interpretation: Model underestimates tail risk; capital multiplier increases above 3.

---

## 6. Python Snippet

```python
import scipy.stats as st

n = 250
p = 0.01

prob_green = st.binom.cdf(4, n, p)
prob_yellow = st.binom.cdf(9, n, p) - prob_green
prob_red = 1 - st.binom.cdf(9, n, p)

print(f"Green zone probability: {prob_green:.4%}")
print(f"Yellow zone probability: {prob_yellow:.4%}")
print(f"Red zone probability: {prob_red:.6%}")
