## 1) The key approximation you quoted

For a **small parallel yield shift** \(\Delta y\), bond PV change is approximated by:

\[
\Delta PV \approx -D_\text{mod}\,PV\,\Delta y \;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,C\,PV\,(\Delta y)^2
\]

- \(D_\text{mod}\): modified duration (in **years**)
- \(C\): convexity (in **years²**)
- Sign: if yields go **up** (\(\Delta y>0\)), PV typically goes **down**.

## 2) Zero-coupon bond: modified duration + convexity

For a zero-coupon bond with maturity \(T\) (years), yield \(y\), priced with annual compounding:
\[
P = \frac{F}{(1+y)^T}
\]

### Modified duration (zero)
\[
D_\text{mod}=\frac{T}{1+y}
\]

### Convexity (zero)
\[
C=\frac{T(T+1)}{(1+y)^2}
\]

### Example: 10Y zero coupon, \(y=4\%\)
- \(T=10\), \(y=0.04\)

\[
D_\text{mod}=\frac{10}{1.04}=9.6154
\]
\[
C=\frac{10\cdot 11}{1.04^2}=\frac{110}{1.0816}=101.71
\]

If \(PV=100\) and \(\Delta y=+25\text{bp}=0.0025\):
\[
\Delta PV \approx -9.6154\cdot100\cdot0.0025 + \frac12\cdot101.71\cdot100\cdot(0.0025)^2
\]
\[
\Delta PV \approx -2.4039 + 0.0318 = -2.3721
\]

## 3) Coupon bond pricing + “delta PV” with coupons

A standard fixed-rate Treasury note/bond pays **semiannual coupons**. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Let:
- face \(F\)
- annual coupon rate \(c\)
- payments per year \(m=2\)
- yield-to-maturity \(y\)
- cashflow times \(t_i\) (years)

### PV with coupons
\[
PV = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{CF_i}{\left(1+\frac{y}{m}\right)^{m t_i}}
\quad,\quad
CF_i=
\begin{cases}
\frac{cF}{m} & i < N \\
\frac{cF}{m}+F & i = N
\end{cases}
\]

### Duration/convexity approximation (same form, now coupons are included)
\[
\Delta PV \approx -D_\text{mod}\,PV\,\Delta y \;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,C\,PV\,(\Delta y)^2
\]

Where \(D_\text{mod}\) and \(C\) are computed from the **coupon cashflows** (not the zero-coupon shortcuts).

## 4) US Treasury example: 6m, 1y, 2y, 5y, 10y, 20y, 30y — shock PnL

### Inputs (real auction data examples)

Assume you buy **$1,000,000 face** of each security near issuance (so we can use auction coupon/yield as a clean example):

- **6M (26-week) bill**: High investment rate ~ **3.586%** (bill is zero-coupon). :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  
- **1Y (52-week) bill**: High investment rate ~ **3.587%** (bill is zero-coupon). :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}  
- **2Y note**: coupon **3.750%**, high yield ~ **3.795%**. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}  
- **5Y note (Dec 2025 issue)**: coupon **3-5/8%**, high yield **3.747%**, price **99.448418**. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}  
- **10Y note (May 2025 issue)**: coupon **4-1/4%**, high yield **4.342%**, price **99.260100**. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}  
- **20Y bond (Nov 2025 issue)**: coupon **4-5/8%**, high yield **4.706%**, price **98.954789**. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}  
- **30Y bond (Nov 2025 issue)**: coupon **4-5/8%**, high yield **4.694%**, price **98.894977**. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}  

### Method used for the table below (clean and reproducible)
- Semiannual compounding for notes/bonds.
- Bills treated as zero-coupon.
- PnL uses the **duration + convexity** approximation:
  \[
  \Delta PV \approx -D_\text{mod}\,PV\,\Delta y + \tfrac12 C\,PV\,(\Delta y)^2
  \]
- Shocks shown are **+25bp** and **+50bp** (rates up → price down).

> Numbers are approximate (ignores exact day-count/accrual and exact cashflow dates), but the ranking and magnitudes are realistic for risk intuition.

### Results (per $1mm face)

| Security | Coupon | Yield used | ModDur (yrs) | Convexity (yrs²) | PnL @ +25bp | PnL @ +50bp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6M Bill | 0.000% | 3.586% | 0.49 | 0.48 | **-$1,205** | **-$2,407** |
| 1Y Bill | 0.000% | 3.587% | 0.98 | 1.45 | **-$2,366** | **-$4,723** |
| 2Y Note | 3.750% | 3.795% | 1.91 | 4.64 | **-$4,754** | **-$9,480** |
| 5Y Note | 3.625% | 3.747% | 4.53 | 23.81 | **-$11,193** | **-$22,238** |
| 10Y Note | 4.250% | 4.342% | 8.07 | 77.37 | **-$19,777** | **-$39,075** |
| 20Y Bond | 4.625% | 4.706% | 12.91 | 220.50 | **-$31,266** | **-$61,168** |
| 30Y Bond | 4.625% | 4.694% | 16.06 | 373.43 | **-$38,545** | **-$74,782** |

### Takeaways
- **Shock PnL scales ~ linearly with duration** for small moves; convexity helps a bit (softens losses when yields rise, boosts gains when yields fall).
- **Long-end risk dominates**: 30Y loses far more than 10Y for the same bp move, per unit notional.
- Bills are low duration (close to time-to-maturity), so their PnL is much smaller.

## 5) Quick “plug-in” version for your desk notes

- If you know PV and modified duration:
  \[
  \Delta PV \approx -D_\text{mod}\cdot PV \cdot \Delta y
  \]
- If you also know convexity:
  \[
  \Delta PV \approx -D_\text{mod}\cdot PV \cdot \Delta y + \frac12 \cdot C \cdot PV \cdot (\Delta y)^2
  \]
- For **DV01**:
  \[
  \text{DV01} \approx D_\text{mod}\cdot PV \cdot 10^{-4}
  \]
