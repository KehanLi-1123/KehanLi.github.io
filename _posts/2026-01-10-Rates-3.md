## 1) Core risk approximation (works for coupon bonds too)

For a **small parallel yield shift** $$\Delta y$$:

$$
\Delta PV \approx -D_{\text{mod}}\;PV\;\Delta y \;+\;\frac{1}{2}\;C\;PV\;(\Delta y)^2
$$

- $$D_{\text{mod}}$$ = modified duration (years)
- $$C$$ = (modified) convexity (years²)
- This formula **already “takes account of coupons”** as long as $$D_{\text{mod}}$$ and $$C$$ are computed from the bond’s coupon cashflows.

### DV01 link
DV01 is the PV change for a **1 bp** move (market convention often quotes it as a positive number):

$$
DV01 \approx D_{\text{mod}}\;PV\;10^{-4}
$$

And for a shock:

$$
\Delta PV \approx -DV01 \times \Delta y_{\text{(in bp)}} 
\quad\text{(1st order only)}
$$

## 2) Zero-coupon (quick closed forms)

For a zero with maturity $$T$$ and annual-compounded yield $$y$$:

$$
D_{\text{mod}}=\frac{T}{1+y}
$$

$$
C=\frac{T(T+1)}{(1+y)^2}
$$

## 3) Coupon bond PV definition (semiannual Treasuries)

US Treasury notes/bonds pay coupons **semiannually**. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Let coupon rate be $$c$$ (annual), face $$F$$, payments per year $$m=2$$, yield $$y$$, payment times $$t_i$$:

$$
PV = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{CF_i}{\left(1+\frac{y}{m}\right)^{m t_i}},
\qquad
CF_i=
\begin{cases}
\frac{cF}{m} & i<N \\
\frac{cF}{m}+F & i=N
\end{cases}
$$

Then you use the same risk approximation in Section 1.
