## 1. Why duration and convexity matter

For a standard fixed-rate bond, **price is a convex function of yield**.  
This implies:

- Price falls when yields rise
- Price rises when yields fall
- The curvature (convexity) cushions losses and enhances gains

Risk management therefore uses a **second-order approximation** of PV changes.

---

## 2. Core pricing-risk approximation (coupon bonds included)

For a small parallel yield shift $$\Delta y$$:

$$
\Delta PV
\;\approx\;
-\,D_{\mathrm{mod}}\cdot PV \cdot \Delta y
\;+\;
\frac{1}{2}\,C\cdot PV \cdot (\Delta y)^2
$$

Where:
- $$PV$$ = present value (market value)
- $$D_{\mathrm{mod}}$$ = modified duration (years)
- $$C$$ = (modified) convexity (years\(^2\))
- $$\Delta y$$ in **decimal form** (25bp = 0.0025)

This formula **fully incorporates coupons** as long as  
$$D_{\mathrm{mod}}$$ and $$C$$ are computed from the actual cashflows.

---

## 3. DV01 (first-order risk)

DV01 is the PV change for a **1bp increase in yield**:

$$
DV01
\;\approx\;
D_{\mathrm{mod}}\cdot PV \cdot 10^{-4}
$$

First-order approximation using DV01:

$$
\Delta PV
\;\approx\;
-\,DV01 \cdot \Delta y_{\text{(in bp)}}
$$

Convexity explains why this linear approximation **breaks down** for large moves.

---

## 4. Percentage PnL formulation

Dividing through by $$PV$$:

$$
\frac{\Delta PV}{PV}
\;\approx\;
-\,D_{\mathrm{mod}}\cdot \Delta y
\;+\;
\frac{1}{2}\,C\cdot (\Delta y)^2
$$

Percentage PnL:

$$
\%\mathrm{PnL}
\;\approx\;
\frac{\Delta PV}{PV}\times 100\%
$$

---

## 5. Why convexity enters with a positive sign

Bond price as a function of yield is **convex**:

$$
\frac{dP}{dy} < 0,
\qquad
\frac{d^2P}{dy^2} > 0
$$

From the Taylor expansion:

$$
P(y+\Delta y)
\approx
P(y)
+ \frac{dP}{dy}\Delta y
+ \frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2P}{dy^2}(\Delta y)^2
$$

Define:

$$
D_{\mathrm{mod}} = -\frac{1}{P}\frac{dP}{dy},
\qquad
C = \frac{1}{P}\frac{d^2P}{dy^2}
$$

Substitution yields the **positive convexity term**.  
Convexity therefore **reduces losses when rates rise** and **amplifies gains when rates fall**.

---

## 6. Coupon bond PV definition (US Treasuries)

US Treasury notes and bonds pay coupons **semiannually**.

$$
PV
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\frac{CF_i}
{\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right)^{2t_i}}
$$

$$
CF_i
=
\begin{cases}
\frac{cF}{2} & i < N \\
\frac{cF}{2}+F & i=N
\end{cases}
$$

Bills are treated as **zero-coupon instruments**.

## 8. Desk-ready takeaways

- **DV01 scales linearly** with duration and PV  
- **Convexity always helps** for plain fixed-rate bonds  
- Long-end Treasuries dominate **rate-shock PnL**
- Duration alone is insufficient beyond small moves

> *Duration is slope. Convexity is curvature. Risk lives in both.*
