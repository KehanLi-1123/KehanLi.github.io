## Introduction

A key challenge in **pension fund risk management** is that the **convexity of liabilities** is much larger than the **convexity of assets**.  
This structural mismatch drives **pro-cyclical hedging flows** and explains why funds are often said to be *short convexity*.

---

## Liability Convexity

- Pension liabilities are the **present value of long-dated cashflows** (benefit payments stretching 20–60 years).  
- Longer maturities imply:
  - **High duration** (often 15–25 years).  
  - **High convexity**, since convexity grows roughly with the square of maturity.  
- Liabilities are **option-free** (participants cannot prepay), so convexity is always **positive and large**.  

Mathematically, for option-free liabilities:

$$
C_L(y) = \frac{\sum t_i^2 \, CF_i \, e^{-y t_i}}{\sum CF_i \, e^{-y t_i}}
$$

This grows quickly as large $$t_i$$ dominate.

---

## Asset Convexity

Pension fund assets are typically more mixed and have **lower convexity**:

- **Bonds**: government/credit portfolios with maturities 5–12 years → convexity is smaller.  
- **Swaps**: receive-fixed swaps extend duration but have lower convexity than true 30y bonds.  
- **Credit/MBS**: callable or prepayable bonds can introduce **negative convexity**.  
- **Equities/Real assets**: no fixed cashflows, often treated as zero convexity in interest rate terms.  

Thus, even if duration is hedged, the convexity mismatch remains.

---

## Empirical Ordering

In practice:

$$
\text{Convexity}_{\text{liabilities}} \;>\; \text{Convexity}_{\text{assets}}
$$

- Liabilities: long-dated, option-free, strongly convex.  
- Assets: shorter maturity, possible negative convexity, and non-bond holdings.  

---

## Consequences for Risk Management

Because **liability convexity dominates**:

- When yields **fall**, liability DV01 rises faster than asset DV01.  
- This **widens the duration gap**.  
- To close the gap, funds must **buy more long bonds/swaps into rallies**.  
- Conversely, when yields **rise**, they must **sell**.  

This **buy-high, sell-low dynamic** is exactly the behavior of a **short-convexity position**.

---

## One-Liner Summary

Empirically, **pension fund liabilities always have higher convexity than assets**.  
This structural convexity gap explains why LDI strategies create **pro-cyclical hedging flows**, amplifying moves in long-term yields.
