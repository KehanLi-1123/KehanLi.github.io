## NII01 for Daily-Reset SOFR, 3M Term SOFR, and 3M Compounded SOFR — Rigorous Comparison

### Setup and Notation

- Valuation (report) date: $$t_0$$  
- Horizon: $$T = t_0 + 1\text{y}$$  
- Notional: $$N$$  
- Discount curve $$P(t)$$ and forward rates $$L(t; T_1, T_2)$$ are derived from the **SOFR curve**  
- Daycount fraction over $$[t_i,t_{i+1})$$: $$\alpha_i = \frac{\text{DC}(t_i,t_{i+1})}{\text{basis}}$$  
- Spread (margin): $$m$$  
- A **parallel 1 bp shock** moves all forward rates by $$\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$$  

The formal definition is:

$$
\text{NII01} = \left.\frac{\partial \text{NII}_{[t_0,T]}}{\partial \varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
$$

We consider three types of 1-year floating-rate loans:

1. **Daily-reset SOFR** — resets and pays interest daily:  
   $$R_i = \text{SOFR}(t_i)$$, cashflow $$N\,\alpha_i\,(R_i + m)$$

2. **3M Term SOFR** — forward-looking rate published today (CME Term SOFR).  
   Each 3-month period $$[u_k, u_{k+1}]$$ has rate fixed **in advance** at $$u_k$$:  
   $$L_k = L(u_k; u_k, u_{k+1})$$, cashflow $$N\,\alpha_{u_k}\,(L_k + m)$$

3. **3M Compounded SOFR (in arrears)** — rate compounded from daily SOFR over each quarter:  
   $$1 + \alpha_{u_k} R_k^{\text{cmp}} = \prod_{d \in [u_k, u_{k+1})} (1 + \alpha_d r_d)$$,  
   paid at $$u_{k+1}$$

---

### Projected NII over the One-Year Horizon

The projected NII over $$[t_0, T]$$ is:

$$
\text{NII}_{[t_0,T]} = \sum_j N\,\alpha_j\,(r^{\text{ref}}_j + m)
$$

Under a **parallel shock** at $$t_0$$:

$$
\frac{\partial \text{NII}_{[t_0,T]}}{\partial \varepsilon}
= N \sum_{j \in \mathcal{U}} \alpha_j \frac{\partial r^{\text{ref}}_j}{\partial \varepsilon}
\approx N \sum_{j \in \mathcal{U}} \alpha_j
$$

where $$\mathcal{U}$$ is the set of **periods whose reference rate is not yet fixed** at $$t_0$$.

- **Daily-reset SOFR:** only today’s accrual is fixed; the rest of the year is unfixed  
  $$\Rightarrow \sum_{j \in \mathcal{U}} \alpha_j \approx 1$$
- **3M Term SOFR:** first quarter is fixed in advance, next three quarters unfixed  
  $$\Rightarrow \sum_{j \in \mathcal{U}} \alpha_j \approx 0.75$$
- **3M Compounded SOFR (in arrears):** accrues daily with realized SOFRs, so behaves like daily reset  
  $$\Rightarrow \sum_{j \in \mathcal{U}} \alpha_j \approx 1$$

Hence:

$$
\text{NII01}^{(\text{daily})} \approx N \times 10^{-4}, \quad
\text{NII01}^{(\text{3M compounded})} \approx N \times 10^{-4}, \quad
\text{NII01}^{(\text{3M term})} \approx 0.75\,N \times 10^{-4}
$$

If NII01 is measured as **projected one-year sensitivity**, then:

$$
\boxed{
\text{NII01}_{\text{Daily SOFR}}
\;\approx\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Compounded SOFR}}
\;>\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Term SOFR}}
}
$$

The more frequently a loan refixes, the larger its projected NII01 exposure, because a larger portion of its future interest is still unfixed.

---

### The “Locked-In / Next-Refix” Convention

Many ALM teams instead define NII01 as the **current exposure until the next refix date** — the “locked-in” convention:

$$
\text{NII01}^{\text{locked}}(t_0)
\propto N \times \frac{\text{Next Refix Date} - t_0}{365}
$$

Then:

- **Daily-reset SOFR:** next refix in ≈ 1 day  
  $$\Rightarrow \frac{1}{365}$$
- **3M Term SOFR:** next refix in ≈ 90 days  
  $$\Rightarrow \frac{90}{365}$$
- **3M Compounded SOFR:** although the coupon pays quarterly, the reference rate compounds daily, so the rate refixes continuously  
  $$\Rightarrow \frac{1}{365}$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{\text{NII01}^{\text{locked}}_{\text{3M Term}}}{\text{NII01}^{\text{locked}}_{\text{Daily}}}
\approx 90,
\qquad
\text{NII01}^{\text{locked}}_{\text{3M compounded}}
\approx
\text{NII01}^{\text{locked}}_{\text{Daily}}
$$

and thus:

$$
\boxed{
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Term SOFR}}
\;>\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{Daily SOFR}}
\;\approx\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Compounded SOFR}}
}
$$

---

### Why the Compounded Rate Behaves Like Daily Reset

For the compounded rate over $$[t, t+\Delta]$$ with daily steps $$\alpha_d$$:

$$
1+\alpha_\Delta\,R^{\text{cmp}}
= \prod_{d=1}^{n}\bigl(1+\alpha_d\,r_d\bigr)
$$

Differentiating with respect to a parallel shock $$\varepsilon$$ gives:

$$
\frac{\partial R^{\text{cmp}}}{\partial \varepsilon}
= \frac{1}{\alpha_\Delta}\sum_{d=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha_d}{1+\alpha_d r_d}
\approx 1
$$

Thus, a +1 bp parallel shift increases the compounded-in-arrears rate **by approximately 1 bp**, just like daily SOFR.  
Any deviation is second-order and negligible for 1 bp shocks.

---

### Reconciling the Two Conventions

| Convention | Definition | Larger NII01 |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| **Projected over 1 year** | Shock applied to all future fixings within the horizon | **Daily SOFR ≈ 3M Compounded SOFR > 3M Term SOFR** |
| **Locked-in / Next-Refix** | Exposure to rate already fixed until next reset | **3M Term SOFR > Daily SOFR ≈ 3M Compounded SOFR** |

---

### Final Conclusion

At the **start date** of the loan:

- If the reference is **3M Term SOFR (forward-looking)**, the first quarter’s rate is **already fixed**, leading to a **larger locked-in NII01**.  
- If the reference is **3M Compounded SOFR (in arrears)**, the rate is **not fixed** but realized daily, effectively behaving like **daily SOFR**.

Therefore, under the **locked-in (refix-date / 365)** convention:

$$
\boxed{
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Term SOFR}}
\;>\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{Daily SOFR}}
\;\approx\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Compounded SOFR}}
}
$$

If instead NII01 is measured as **projected sensitivity over the 1-year horizon**, the ordering reverses:

$$
\boxed{
\text{NII01}_{\text{Daily SOFR}}
\;\approx\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Compounded SOFR}}
\;>\;
\text{NII01}_{\text{3M Term SOFR}}
}
$$

**In summary:**  
- **Term SOFR (forward-looking)** ⇒ first period fixed ⇒ **larger locked-in NII01**  
- **Compounded SOFR (in arrears)** ⇒ effectively daily reset ⇒ **same NII01 as daily SOFR**
