Carr–Madan pricing is often described as an FFT technique.  
This is misleading.
The real innovation is **not the FFT** — it is the realization that option prices must first be made **Fourier-transformable**.
Once integrability is restored, the FFT is simply a computational accelerator.

> **Core idea:** Carr–Madan converts option pricing from a density problem into a characteristic-function problem.

---

# 1. The Classical Starting Point — Risk-Neutral Pricing

Under the risk-neutral measure, a European call satisfies

$$
C(K) = e^{-rT} E[(S_T-K)^+]
$$

Introduce log variables:

$$
x = \ln S_T, \quad k = \ln K
$$

Then

$$
C(k)= e^{-rT}
\int_k^\infty (e^x-e^k) f(x)\,dx
$$

where $$f(x)$$ is the density of the log-price.

This is the traditional framework:

$$
\text{Price}=\int \text{payoff} \times \text{density}
$$

It works beautifully when the density is known — but many modern models do **not** provide a closed-form density.

---

# 2. The Shift Toward Characteristic Functions

Instead of working with densities, Fourier methods use the characteristic function:

$$
\phi(u)=E[e^{iu\ln S_T}]
$$

For example, under Black–Scholes:

$$
\ln S_T \sim N(m,\sigma^2T)
$$

with

$$
m = \ln S_0 + (r-\tfrac12\sigma^2)T
$$

giving

$$
\phi(u)=\exp\left(iu m - \tfrac12\sigma^2 u^2 T\right)
$$

---

## Why Characteristic Functions Are Powerful

Characteristic functions possess a critical property:

> **They always exist.**

Even when the density is unknown, irregular, or analytically intractable, the characteristic function often remains available.

This is why Fourier pricing dominates models such as:

- Heston  
- Variance Gamma  
- Bates  
- CGMY  
- General Lévy processes  

The density may be elusive — the characteristic function is not.

---

# 3. When Does a Fourier Transform Exist?

The Fourier transform of a function $$f(k)$$ is

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ivk} f(k)\, dk
$$

For the transform to exist in the ordinary sense, the integral must converge to a **single finite value**.

A useful sufficient condition:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |f(k)| dk < \infty
$$

In plain language:

> The function must decay fast enough.

---

# 4. The Hidden Structural Problem with Call Prices

Consider deep in-the-money calls.

As strike approaches zero:

$$
k \to -\infty
$$

the call becomes nearly identical to holding the stock:

$$
C(k) \to S_0
$$

The function does **not decay**.

Now inspect the transform:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ivk} C(k) dk
\approx
S_0 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ivk} dk
$$

Evaluate a truncated version:

$$
I(L)=\int_{-L}^{L} S_0 e^{ivk} dk
= S_0 \frac{e^{ivL}-e^{-ivL}}{iv}
= S_0 \frac{2\sin(vL)}{v}
$$

---

## Does This Converge?

As $$L\to\infty$$:

- $$\sin(vL)$$ oscillates indefinitely  
- The integral is bounded  
- But it **never approaches a single value**

Therefore,

$$
\lim_{L\to\infty} I(L)
$$

does not exist.

---

## Critical Mathematical Insight

**Bounded does NOT imply convergence.**

For example:

$$
\sin(L)
$$

remains between −1 and 1, yet

$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\sin(L)
$$

does not exist.

This behavior is known as a **non-convergent oscillatory integral**.

---

# 5. Geometric Intuition — Why the Transform Fails

Imagine plotting the call price against log-strike.

### Left Tail (Deep ITM)

The curve flattens toward a constant:

> a horizontal asymptote at $$S_0$$.

Now picture multiplying by the complex wave:

$$
e^{ivk} = \cos(vk) + i\sin(vk)
$$

You are integrating:

> a constant amplitude × an infinite oscillation.

Geometrically, the positive and negative areas never settle into a stable cancellation.

The integral keeps “sloshing” forever.

No limit forms.

---

# 6. Carr–Madan’s Breakthrough — Exponential Damping

Carr and Madan applied a deceptively simple idea:

Tilt the function downward.

Define the damped call:

$$
\tilde C(k)=e^{\alpha k} C(k), \quad \alpha>0
$$

Now as

$$
k\to -\infty
$$

we have

$$
e^{\alpha k}\to 0
$$

and therefore

$$
\tilde C(k)\to 0
$$

The function becomes integrable.

---

## Geometric Interpretation

Without damping:

```
oscillation × constant height → infinite wobble
```

With damping:

```
oscillation × shrinking height → settles to a limit
```

You have effectively rotated the function into a decaying direction.

This is the true intellectual leap of Carr–Madan.

> **Not FFT — integrability engineering.**

---

# 7. Fourier Transform of the Damped Call

Define

$$
\psi(v)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
e^{ivk}\tilde C(k)\,dk
$$

Carr–Madan derived:

$$
\psi(v)
=
e^{-rT}
\frac{
\phi(v-(\alpha+1)i)
}{
\alpha^2+\alpha-v^2+i(2\alpha+1)v
}
$$

Notice something profound:

> The option price is now expressed purely via the characteristic function.

No density appears anywhere.

---

# 8. Where Does the Denominator Come From? (Intuition Only)

The payoff contains exponentials:

$$
e^x - e^k
$$

Multiplying by the damping term shifts the argument of the characteristic function into the **complex plane**:

$$
u \rightarrow v-(\alpha+1)i
$$

Mathematically, this is equivalent to evaluating the transform along a **parallel contour** in complex space.

The denominator arises from integrating exponentials — essentially a rational function produced by repeated division by linear terms.

You do **not** need to memorize it.

Understand the mechanism:

> damping ⇒ complex shift ⇒ rational kernel.

---

# 9. Recovering the Option Price

The inverse transform gives

$$
C(k)
=
\frac{e^{-\alpha k}}{\pi}
\int_0^\infty
\text{Re}
\left(
e^{-ivk}\psi(v)
\right)
dv
$$

### Why Only the Real Part?

Although the machinery is complex-valued:

- Imaginary components cancel
- The final price is always real

Complex numbers are merely a computational scaffold.

---

# 10. FFT — The Accelerator, Not the Idea

After discretization:

$$
v_j=j\eta
$$

$$
\lambda=\frac{2\pi}{N\eta}
$$

$$
k_j=-\frac{N\lambda}{2}+j\lambda
$$

we obtain

$$
C(k_j)
\approx
\frac{e^{-\alpha k_j}}{\pi}
\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}
e^{-iv_m k_j}\psi(v_m)\eta
$$

This summation is exactly what the **Fast Fourier Transform** computes efficiently.

---

## What FFT Actually Buys You

One transform produces:

- thousands of strikes simultaneously  
- extremely fast calibration  
- efficient volatility surface construction  

This is why Fourier pricing is foundational in modern derivatives desks.

---

# 11. The Deep Insight Most Quants Miss

Carr–Madan is frequently taught backwards.

People focus on FFT.

But the real intellectual hierarchy is:

1. Enforce integrability  
2. Express price via characteristic function  
3. Apply Fourier inversion  
4. Use FFT for speed  

> **The FFT is the last step — not the breakthrough.**
