Bond futures are some of the most liquid and widely used instruments in global fixed income markets. They provide a standardized way to hedge or speculate on government bond yields without directly trading the underlying bonds.

---

## 📌 What is a Bond Future?

A **bond future** is a standardized futures contract where the underlying is a government bond (or a basket of deliverable bonds).  

- **Agreement**: Buy/sell a notional bond at a future date and price.  
- **Underlying**: Not one specific bond, but a **set of eligible government bonds** defined by maturity buckets.  
- **Settlement**: Delivery occurs during the **contract’s delivery month** (e.g., March, June, September, December).  
- **Use cases**:  
  - Hedge interest rate risk  
  - Take directional views on yields  
  - Arbitrage futures vs. cash bonds  

---

## ⚙️ How Bond Futures Work

1. **Delivery Month (T)**  
   - Futures expire in quarterly cycles (Mar, Jun, Sep, Dec).  
   - The **delivery period** runs from the first to last business day of that month.  
   - The **short** chooses when to deliver within the window.  

2. **Deliverable Basket**  
   - Each contract specifies eligible bonds (e.g., “remaining maturity 8.5–10.5 years”).  
   - Example: The 10y U.S. Treasury future allows delivery of any Treasury note with **6.5–10 years** to maturity.  

3. **Conversion Factor (CF)**  
   - Adjusts for different coupons/maturities in the basket.  
   - Futures invoice price =  

     $$
     \text{Futures Price} \times \text{CF} \times \$100{,}000 + \text{Accrued Interest}
     $$  

4. **Cheapest-to-Deliver (CTD)**  
   - The short will always choose the bond that minimizes delivery cost.  
   - Market participants track CTD and **implied repo rates** closely.

## 🔑 Examples of Standard Contracts

### U.S. Treasury Futures (CME)
- **2y (ZT)**: 1y9m–2y remaining maturity, notional \$200k  
- **5y (ZF)**: 4y2m–5y3m, notional \$100k  
- **10y (ZN)**: 6.5–10y, notional \$100k  
- **Ultra 10y (TN)**: 9.5–10y  
- **30y (ZB)**: 15–25y  
- **Ultra 30y (UB)**: 25y+  

### Euro Government Bond Futures (Eurex)
- **Schatz**: 1.75–2.25y  
- **Bobl**: 4.5–5.5y  
- **Bund**: 8.5–10.5y  
- **Buxl**: 24–35y  

### UK Gilt Futures (ICE Europe)
- **Long Gilt**: 8.75–13y  

### Japan (TSE)
- **10y JGB Future**: 7–11y, notional ¥100m

## 📊 Why “5y to 15y”?

When people say most bond futures are between **5–15 years**, they mean:  

- It refers to the **tenor of the underlying bonds** eligible for delivery (not the futures expiry).  
- The 5–15y maturity segment is the **global benchmark zone**:  
  - Deep liquidity  
  - Sensitive to monetary policy  
  - Central to duration hedging  

Futures outside this zone (2y, 30y, etc.) exist, but 5–15y contracts dominate trading volumes.

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- A bond future is a **derivative on a basket of government bonds**.  
- The contract expires in a **delivery month**, but the underlying deliverable bonds have **longer maturities (5–15y is most common)**.  
- Pricing involves **conversion factors** and the concept of **cheapest-to-deliver**.  
- They are essential tools for **hedging, speculation, and arbitrage** in fixed income markets.
