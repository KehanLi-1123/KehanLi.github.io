## Summary

This post shows how to go from **observed bond prices** to:

- **Discount factors** $$P(t)$$
- **Zero (spot) rates** $$R(0,T)$$
- **Par (coupon) rates** $$c_T$$
- **Forward rates** (simple and continuous)

It also highlights the difference between **on-coupon-date** vs **off-cycle (accrued)** calculations and the link to **par swap rates**.

---

## Notation

- $$P(t_i)$$: discount factor to time $$t_i$$ (PV of £1 paid at $$t_i$$).
- $$\Delta_i$$: year-fraction for $$(t_{i-1}, t_i]$$ under the chosen day-count.
- $$c_T$$: **annual** coupon rate of a maturity $$T$$ bond.
- $$A_T := \sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_i\,P(t_i)$$: **fixed-leg annuity** (a.k.a. PVBP/annuity).
- $$D$$: **dirty** price (clean + accrued).
- $$\alpha$$: accrued year-fraction since the last coupon date.

---

## Core identities

### Bond pricing (dirty)

For a unit-notional fixed-coupon bond with coupons $c_T \Delta_i$ and redemption $1$ at $T$:

$$
D \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} c_T\,\Delta_i\,P(t_i) \;+\; P(T)
\;=\; c_T\,A_T \;+\; P(T).
$$

### Bootstrapping a new DF

If all earlier discount factors are known, a bond maturing at $t_N$ implies:

$$
P(t_N) \;=\; \frac{D - \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} c\,\Delta_i\,P(t_i)}{1 + c\,\Delta_N}.
$$

*(With true zero-coupon instruments/STRIPS you directly have $P(T)=\text{Dirty}/\text{Redemption}$.)*

### Zero (spot) rate

Choose a compounding convention (continuous is common in analytics):

$$
R(0,T) \;=\; -\frac{1}{T}\,\ln P(T),
\qquad
R^{(1)}(0,T) \;=\; P(T)^{-1/T}-1 \quad \text{(annual comp).}
$$

### Forwards between $T_1$ and $T_2$ ($\Delta=T_2-T_1$)

$$
f^{c}_{T_1,T_2} \;=\; -\frac{\ln\!\big(P(T_2)/P(T_1)\big)}{\Delta},
\qquad
f^{\text{simple}}_{T_1,T_2} \;=\; \frac{P(T_1)/P(T_2)-1}{\Delta}.
$$

## Par (coupon) rate

### On a coupon date (accrued $=0$)

Target **price = 1** (par) and use the dirty-price equation:

$$
1 \;=\; c_T\,A_T + P(T)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\,c_T \;=\; \dfrac{1 - P(T)}{A_T}\,}.
$$

This is **identical** to the **par swap rate** formula, since the fixed-leg PV is $c_T A_T$ and the floating-leg PV is $1-P(T)$.

### Off a coupon date (clean = 1 target)

If settlement is not a coupon date, **clean** and **dirty** differ by accrued interest $c_T\,\alpha$.
Want **clean = 1** $\Rightarrow$ **dirty** must satisfy $D = 1 + c_T\,\alpha$.

Plug into the PV:

$$
c_T\,A_T + P(T) \;=\; 1 + c_T\,\alpha
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\,c_T \;=\; \dfrac{1 - P(T)}{A_T - \alpha}\,}.
$$

*(If instead you target **dirty = 1** off-cycle, use the on-coupon formula.)*

---

## Worked example — semiannual coupons (on a coupon date)

Assume maturity $T=3$ years with semiannual payments ($\Delta_i = 0.5$). Suppose:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0.5)&=0.990,\quad P(1.0)=0.980,\quad P(1.5)=0.968,\\
P(2.0)&=0.955,\quad P(2.5)=0.940,\quad P(3.0)=0.924.
\end{aligned}
$$

Annuity:

$$
A_T \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{6} 0.5\,P(t_i)
      \;=\; 0.5\times(0.990+0.980+0.968+0.955+0.940+0.924)
      \;=\; 2.8785.
$$

Numerator: $1 - P(3.0) = 0.076$.

**Par coupon (annualized):**

$$
c_T \;=\; \frac{1 - P(T)}{A_T}
     \;=\; \frac{0.076}{2.8785}
     \;\approx\; \mathbf{2.64\%}.
$$

Each semiannual coupon is $c_T \Delta = 2.64\%\times 0.5 \approx \mathbf{1.32\%}$.

---

## Off-cycle variant (clean = 1 target)

If settlement is 2 months after the last coupon, take an illustrative accrued fraction $\alpha\approx 2/12=0.1667$ (use your day-count):

$$
c_T \;=\; \frac{1-P(T)}{A_T-\alpha}
     \;=\; \frac{0.076}{2.8785-0.1667}
     \;\approx\; \mathbf{2.74\%}.
$$

Because the clean price excludes accrued, the coupon must be a touch **higher** to keep clean $=1$.

---

## Practical checklist (pitfalls to avoid)

- **Use dirty price** in the valuation equation (clean + accrued).
- **Day-count & frequency** must be consistent for $\Delta_i$ and $\alpha$ (ACT/365F, 30/360, etc.).
- **Bootstrapping order**: move from shortest maturities to longest so earlier DFs are known.
- **Mixed instruments**: combine bills/notes, FRAs, swaps, and STRIPS to fill the curve—math is the same: each instrument adds one equation in unknown DFs.
- **Interpolation**: prefer monotone methods in **log-DF** space for stability; for IFR $f(0,t)$ you need differentiability of $\ln P(t)$.
- **Par vs YTM**: par rate is implied by the **DF curve**; **YTM** is a single-rate summary and will generally differ unless the bond is exactly at par and the curve is flat under that compounding.

## Appendix: quick formulas

- **Bootstrap last DF** (coupon bond maturing at $t_N$):

  $$
  P(t_N) \;=\; \frac{D - \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} c\,\Delta_i\,P(t_i)}{1 + c\,\Delta_N}.
  $$

- **Zero (continuous)**: $R(0,T) = -\dfrac{1}{T}\ln P(T)$.

- **Par (on coupon date)**: $c_T = \dfrac{1 - P(T)}{A_T}$.

- **Par (clean = 1 off-cycle)**: $c_T = \dfrac{1 - P(T)}{A_T - \alpha}$.

- **Forward (continuous)**: $f^{c}_{T_1,T_2} = -\dfrac{\ln\!\big(P(T_2)/P(T_1)\big)}{T_2-T_1}$.

- **Forward (simple)**: $f^{\text{simple}}_{T_1,T_2} = \dfrac{P(T_1)/P(T_2)-1}{T_2-T_1}$.
